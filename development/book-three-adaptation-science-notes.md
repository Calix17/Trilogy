# Book III — science and ethics checks for the adaptation proposal

24 September 2026. Research notes for the writer, not explanatory passages for the novel. Sources checked during this update. The colony organisms, future transfers, and bodily adaptations remain fictional.

## Gene editing is not uniformly unethical

WHO distinguishes somatic editing from heritable editing and identifies greater safety and ethical concerns for inherited interventions. The FDA approved the CRISPR-based treatment Casgevy in 2023. Therefore, “changing DNA is currently unethical” is too broad. The novel's harder questions concern uncertain effects, consent, children and descendants, access, imposed changes, and the option of remaining unmodified. Permission for a present-day treatment does not establish that radical future changes are safe or justified.

Sources: [WHO — Human genome editing](https://www.who.int/health-topics/human-genome-editing); [FDA — first gene therapies for sickle cell disease, 8 December 2023](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-gene-therapies-treat-patients-sickle-cell-disease).

## Mineralized organisms and airborne fibers

Research by Trembath-Reichert and colleagues documents silica biomineralization in terrestrial plants. NCI describes persistent inhaled asbestos fibers, inflammation and scarring, and typically long delays before asbestos-related disease appears. These support separate analogies: organisms can form mineral structures, and some persistent respirable fibers can harm lungs. They do not demonstrate that a plant releasing airborne asbestos is real or that ordinary plant silica is equivalent to asbestos.

For the fiction, a plant-like organism shedding hazardous mineralized fibers is an extrapolation. Specify the relevant fictional property only as far as the plot needs. Chronic exposure can motivate adaptation; a sudden extinction emergency needs a separate acute cause or failure, not overnight asbestos-related cancer. Filters and shelters are meaningful options whose limits need a cause.

Sources: [Trembath-Reichert et al., 2015, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1500289112](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25825729/); [NCI — Asbestos Exposure and Cancer Risk](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/substances/asbestos/asbestos-fact-sheet).

## Ancient mining and evolutionary time

The author has resolved the character chronology: the Instance and Haven's mining history predate Daniel's birth by vast periods. The biological interval is separate. Models of the emergence of intelligence remain debated; the Mills et al. reassessment emphasizes environmental windows and planetary/biological coevolution. Earth's history is a comparison, not a universal stopwatch.

For the writer, this means preserving a sufficiently developed history rather than asserting that any specified number of millions of years guarantees civilization. Multiple seeded environments offer narrative possibilities, not a measured probability. No accelerated-evolution mechanism is selected. The story can reveal one successful lineage without claiming that every dispersed microbe establishes life or that every lineage becomes technological.

Sources: [Mills et al., 2025, Science Advances, doi:10.1126/sciadv.ads5698](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11827626/); [Spiegel and Turner, 2011, Bayesian analysis of life's early emergence](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.3835). The latter concerns abiogenesis inference from limited evidence, not a direct estimate of success for an engineered seeding program.

## Boundaries to preserve in the story

Alien infection requires an assumed biochemical interaction; physical damage or toxicity does not require an organism to reproduce inside human cells. The exact alien biology is unselected, so this is a design distinction rather than a claim about known extraterrestrial pathogens.

An engineered trait can help with a particular exposure without granting general immunity or reconstructing a whole adult body instantly. The capacities and costs of future adaptations are stipulated technology to define privately. A changed genome alone does not determine moral personhood, and “posthuman” need not mean a biological species boundary has been proven.

Preservation through simulation and later instantiation is the author's fictional route. Current science does not establish that either can preserve human consciousness. The novel's continuity rules and the evidence available to its characters should stay consistent with Books I and II while leaving machinery largely unexplained.

These notes support believable choices. They do not require additional scientific exposition in Book I or turn the third book into a technical report.
