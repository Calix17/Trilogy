# The Final Decision — v6 contact and missing-page strategy

24 September 2026. Editorial recommendation for author review, incorporating the latest proposals. This does not select new canon or authorize the narrative render. V5 remains the reading edition. The author’s message is preserved verbatim in the planning source archive.

## Recommended division of responsibility

Give the Institute the public support campaign, and the serpent-associated intermediaries the physical page and one private contact. Keep the woman’s encounter uncertain. This is the recommended alternative to two rival advertising campaigns; the earlier option remains in the record but is not the preferred staging.

| Element | Proposed private source | Purpose | What Book I establishes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| “You don’t have to carry this alone” ads, chapters 1–2 | Institute-associated foundation | Offer support; later discourage a potentially damaging pursuit | A plausible campaign connected to Elias’s searches, not proof that someone reads his mind |
| Car outside the home, chapter 5 | Local intermediary working with the serpent | Deliver the completing page | An unfamiliar car; its occupant and intention are unconfirmed |
| Mailbox envelope, chapter 6 | Same effort | Put the missing information in a possible carrier’s hands | The page resembles the private notebook and later completes its sequence |
| One brief nighttime call, chapter 7 | Serpent-associated intermediary | Direct Elias toward the Institute as a way to see Daniel | An unidentified caller claims the Institute can help; the claim is not narration confirming Daniel’s location |
| Hesk’s contact, chapters 7–8 | Institute | Respond to Elias identifying himself; assess and prepare him | A guarded, clinically plausible invitation to talk, not an immediate promise of crossing |
| Woman near the parking structure, optional chapter 5 beat | Not assigned to a faction | Show uncertainty about what Elias hears | His impression of a phrase; no reliable address, instruction, or proof about Daniel |
| Sami’s ant song, chapter 7 | Sami’s own invention | Show Elias reading private significance into ordinary life | The ant and breadcrumb supply an innocent, observable context |

The ads should not change into threatening personal messages. Their care can be sincere while the Institute withholds information and prefers Elias to remain with his family. A search may trigger general support; personal knowledge should follow his identifiable inquiry. The two groups do not both need access to every part of his life.

## The Institute’s apparent contradiction

An invitation to talk is not an invitation to cross. Hesk can want Elias to attend a consultation, hear him out, and discourage further pursuit. The unknown caller can point to the same building because it also controls access to Daniel. Neither group has to change its objective to explain the shared destination.

Hesk should give useful, bounded answers. She may acknowledge knowing Daniel while declining to promise an encounter until she understands what Elias wants and he understands what it could cost. She should not deny an established fact merely to prolong the mystery. Her eventual cooperation follows from respecting his informed choice within the available opening, not from forgetting an absolute prohibition.

Keep Haven’s protective preference distinct from categorical imprisonment. The Institute can slow and question the search without covertly running out the clock so that Elias has no choice. Its people need not understand the page’s planetary significance.

## The woman: recovered scene and proposed revision

The earlier candidate is in `07-scene-workshop/paranoia-scenes-v1.md`, section 6, “The Door.” She says: “He found the door. You know. Your father.” The paragraph initially reports this clearly, then asks whether Elias assembled it from traffic and a thank-you. This scene is a workshop candidate, not a passage in the current v5 manuscript.

If retained, establish uncertainty at the first hearing. A bus, an interrupted phrase, and Elias’s attention can leave him unsure what was said; do not first certify the exact sentence and later withdraw that certainty. Do not repeat the full advertising slogan here as well as in Sami’s scene. Let her remain a particular ordinary person, not a stock oracle whose homelessness signifies access to hidden truth.

Use this as a short encounter within chapter 5, separated from the dream and car sighting by ordinary action. It should supply no indispensable clue. If it makes that chapter a procession of omens, omit it from the compact pilot. The car and page already provide a concrete outside intervention.

## One private contact is enough

Place the call late in chapter 7, after the ant-song consequence and Elias’s identifiable inquiry to the foundation, before the first Institute visit. It reframes an existing lead instead of introducing another mysterious institution. The private effort may notice his renewed pursuit; it does not need omniscient knowledge of his appointment or final decision.

One possible line is: “Ask them about Daniel.” A slightly more explicit alternative is: “They know where your father is.” These are wording studies, not selected dialogue. Give the caller one practical purpose and no faction name, cosmic explanation, or guaranteed rescue. Do not impersonate Daniel or identify this voice with the dream voice unless the author deliberately chooses that additional plot.

Let Elias ask a natural question or refuse an instruction. A brief exchange is more credible than a succession of enigmatic declarations followed by hang-ups. He can compare the caller’s claim against the address or contact he already found; he does not have to obey solely because a stranger spoke.

With this call, omit the optional unsigned “I know who you are” message. The page already implies private knowledge. No second car delivery, father-specific rival ad, or repeated nighttime caller is needed. Preserve the recital as the cost of Elias’s continued choices, not another perfectly timed intervention.

## Openings and the family timeline

The author has now explicitly proposed an opening every ten years, or more generally limited openings. Earlier notes forbade inferring a regular cycle from the two known opportunities; that remains a useful distinction between an established fact and this new proposal.

Recommendation: use the ten-year spacing as the working private chronology for Daniel’s two return opportunities. In Book I, establish the present opening and its deadline without promising an eternally precise schedule or explaining the technology.

| Time in Elias’s chronology | Proposed event | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Year 0; Elias about 15 | Daniel leaves | Established history; departure is not counted as one of his later return opportunities |
| Year 10; Elias about 25 | First chance to return; Daniel stays. He sends Ruth the personal letter during that opening | Letter date is established; aligning the first return opportunity and letter is the new recommendation |
| Year 20; Elias about 35 | Elias crosses; Daniel has his second chance to return and again stays | Present second opportunity and refusal are established; staging remains to be written |

This ties the letter to a lost opportunity without making it a suicide note or a recruitment message. Daniel’s responsibility remains his choice to stay. The working restriction on another outward journey after returning is compatible with two chances to end one continuous stay.

The intermediaries can know that an opening is approaching and look for a willing candidate. They cannot know that Elias will cross. Their intervention tries to create a delivery opportunity. Avoid a countdown beginning so early that it compresses the caregiving, recital, and grief into an implausible few hours. The short decision window belongs to the actual visit and return choice.

## What Daniel and Elias each bring

Recommend ordinary copies of Daniel’s incomplete material over perfect recollection of precise maps. The original notebook stays with Ruth, preserving Elias’s childhood coloring and later discovery. Daniel can have taken copies of the earlier pages when he left. These are separate objects; no explanation of how paper travels through the crossing is required.

The old material may have arrived at Daniel’s university before Elias colored the notebook. Daniel never received the completing segment. Elias later carries the original notebook and the new page. Father and son recognize the relationship together in chapter 14: Daniel contributes years of interpretation, Elias the missing relation and his own reasoning. Haven verifies the destination and its significance in chapter 15. The page does not bypass the reunion or do all the intellectual work for them.

For the private chronology, the simplest proposed reason for the delay is that the final segment only recently reached the local intermediaries. Do not make them knowingly withhold a complete usable map for twenty years without a motive. The precise cause of the delayed information can remain for later development; it need not become Book I exposition.

Retain the proposed limitation that intrusion into a small part of the Instance is not reliable direct access to Haven’s external attention. The route is through someone who will actually reach and speak with her. Do not establish that Hesk has already received and forwarded the final page. Haven’s earlier knowledge can derive from Daniel’s incomplete report; there must not also be a complete independent message making Elias unnecessary. These are proposed private constraints, not claims that a particular security mechanism has been selected.

## Effect on the mystery and review status

The reader can first group the ads, woman, car, page, and caller into one threatening pattern. Later, Hesk’s reluctance conflicts with the caller’s encouragement. The page proves useful without proving every interpretation Elias made. His father remains his reason to go; carrying a message is the hidden use others make of his search.

Book I need not name the competing group or explain its intrusion. It should nevertheless show enough distinct behavior that the later explanation clarifies the evidence rather than replaces it. The physical letter, encounter, and page do not independently prove an interstellar journey: each can also belong to an ordinary institutional setting. The narrative continues to report Elias’s experience closely.

Review before rendering: attribution of the call; private ten-year chronology; copied-page possession history; delayed arrival of the completion; and whether the optional woman scene earns its space. Exact caller wording and transfer mechanics need not be settled to approve the overall direction. No v6 prose or PDF has been produced in this update.
