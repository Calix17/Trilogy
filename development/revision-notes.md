# The Final Decision — Book I v6 revision record

24 September 2026. Implemented after the author’s “Ok lets go a head.”

V6 is a revised narrative pilot of seventeen chapters, 15,905 chapter-body words and 58 PDF pages including the frontispiece. It preserves the earlier editions and incorporates the accumulated directions in actual prose. The bible, main character sheets, and current synopsis now agree with this edition. The two Book II outline/summary sections remain unchanged.

## The revision’s center

The search now has a clearer sequence of causes while the nature of the encounter remains uncertain. A published paper supplies the coauthor; a parked car precedes the anonymous completing page; one private call directs Elias toward an Institute already contacted through the support campaign. Some evidence is material, some is reported, and some is Elias’s interpretation. The ant song and the bird are ordinary life.

Ruth releases the letter herself. Her fear has a cost, as Daniel’s work has a cost. Neither parent becomes a simple verdict on protection or exploration. The letter, shared grief, father–son work, farewell, and final necklace carry the movement toward forgiveness. Elias need not explain that movement to anyone at breakfast.

Daniel’s full attention is the reunion’s temptation. Their joint interpretation accomplishes something, and the habitat work is a life Elias could want. He gives up time with his father and a meaningful future rather than merely rejecting an obvious mistake. Daniel asks to pursue the possible planet; Elias remains free to return.

Haven’s long account has room to offer a world to imagine. Instance, Library, and Vessel preserve complementary possibilities. The Library includes ecological and cultural inheritance as well as DNA. A record is not automatically a recoverable person. The story gives the Ark’s purpose and way of life without explaining the crossing or providing a component manual.

## Drafting choices made within the authorized direction

- Chose habitat systems as Elias’s aerospace focus, without inventing an employer or a separate technical subplot.
- Focused Daniel’s professional history on quantum measurement and later apparent anomalies. The coauthor’s plural concern is retained; his opinion does not prove the later research false or establish a diagnosis.
- Kept the street woman’s words uncertain from the first hearing. Added neither a confirmed faction identity nor a repeated threatening encounter.
- Used one general support campaign and one private call, rather than competing sets of advertisements. Hesk stops her campaign when asked.
- Kept the childhood visit’s television and family interaction but removed the explicit institutional label. The current searches also omit mental institutions, without narrating why.
- Used copies for Daniel’s partial map and a newly arriving sheet for Elias. Their joint work precedes Haven’s verification.
- Aligned the first return opportunity with the letter ten years after departure. The next opportunity is the present one. The recurring timetable remains unspecified.
- Used a blue keepsake box, an affectionate letter without a guaranteed return promise, a gray stone with a silver grain, and a house-to-rocket toast recurrence.
- Kept the plain room and continuous sensory change. Retired the opening star dome, new-body account, and visible portal machinery.
- Retired Rana’s sister conversation and Haven’s closing instruction to enjoy family life. Their behavior remains useful; Elias makes his own choice.

## Questions deliberately left for later work

Book I does not identify the map’s individual sender, the car’s occupant, the caller, Hesk’s full identity, or how information enters the Instance. The writer-level source remains Sower’s embodied Ark, to be disclosed in Book III. The exact interruption or delay in the original message still needs a private later-series account; this draft does not claim intermediaries possessed a complete message and withheld it for twenty years.

The access restriction is clear in the encounter; its technological cause, global schedule, communication limits, and application to later characters remain undescribed. The writer should settle any later exception before using one. Longevity away from Ark care and the details of Sami’s departure require Book III development. Elias’s retired second visit has not been reinstated.

Daniel asks to travel toward the planet. Whether he ultimately settles, continues aboard, or seeks another threshold remains open. The family’s knowledge after Elias returns is not established by the closing conversation. Gratitude and forgiveness are actions and an emotional direction, not a declaration that every hurt is repaired.

The crossing’s ultimate interpretation and the sound’s source remain intentionally open within Book I. The narration supplies neither a clinical diagnosis nor a factual confirmation of a vision, drug, NDE, or shared illness. Later books can supply more science-fiction history without contradicting these experiences.

This is a completed v6 pilot revision, not a claim that the trilogy is finished or ready for publication. Book II still needs its concrete conspiracy sequence and rescue timing developed; Book III’s bodies, departure, and mortality scenes need their own prose pass.
