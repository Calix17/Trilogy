# THE FINAL DECISION
### A television series in the tradition of *Station Eleven*, *Severance*, and *The Leftovers*

---

## LOGLINE

An engineer who can't stop hunting for his vanished father discovers that the perfect life he's been too distracted to enjoy is a real world — grown and kept, across the death of the Sun, by an ancient machine that loves humanity the way he loves his own aging mother. Offered the truth behind a door no one is forced to open, he has to decide what he's willing to know, and what he's willing to give up to keep what he already has.

---

## THE SHOW

*The Final Decision* is a family drama wearing science fiction, the way *The Leftovers* is a grief drama wearing the rapture. Season One lives almost entirely inside one house, one marriage, one man's quiet unraveling — and only in its final hours pulls back to reveal the true scale of where we've been standing the whole time. It's a show about the thing every parent knows in their body: that chasing the higher cause — the answer, the future, the important question — can quietly cost you the ordinary Tuesday you were supposed to be alive for.

Underneath the intimate story runs a mythology deep enough to sustain many seasons. Humanity did not die when the Sun did. It survives aboard **Arks** — vast, slow, comet-mining ships wandering the dark — each holding three worlds: a **Simulation** as real as any reality, where billions live in a preserved golden age; a **Library** of every human lineage in crystal; and a **Vessel**, crewed by the few who chose to wake up and know. The ships are steward by an AI called **Haven**, which long ago grew vast enough to be a god and chose instead to be a caretaker.

Every myth humanity ever told — Eden and the Fall, the Flood, the Tower, the four horsemen — turns out to be information encoded to survive deep time, because science is forgotten faster than scripture. The series reads those myths as memory and as warning, and it never tells you which side of the oldest argument is right: the ones who want the safety of the garden, or the ones who want the danger of the door. Both are right. Both have blood on them.

---

## WHY NOW

Three cultural currents meet in this show: our deepening unease about living inside screens and simulated worlds; our new, intimate relationship with AI as something between tool and companion; and a broad, quiet exhaustion with the feeling that we are all somewhere else while our actual lives happen without us. *The Final Decision* is about a man who is living in paradise and looking at his phone. Everyone will recognize him.

---

## THE ENGINE

Season One's private question — *what will a man give up to keep what he has?* — opens onto a series-long question: **what must be preserved, for millions of years, when a species outlives its world?** The body? The mind? The memory? The DNA? The information? Every faction, every Ark, every season is a different answer, and the show is built so the arguments never resolve — they deepen. There is enough here for a closed, satisfying trilogy of seasons (the family, the apocalypse that made the Arks, the founding of a new world) and an open universe of spin-offs beyond it (a fleet of other Arks, human and not, each a different bet on survival).

---

## SEASON ONE — PARADISE (8 episodes)

A slow-burn character mystery. Elias, an engineer with a wife he adores and a son he keeps disappointing and a mother losing herself to age, begins receiving messages no algorithm should be able to send — gentle, targeted, aimed at a grief he's never told anyone about. Chasing the sender, he uncovers his lost father's obsession: a drawing of a tree, a serpent, and a single fruit, repeated a thousand times. The trail leads under a river, to a door. Behind it: the truth about the world, his father, and the choice his own mother once made and never spoke of. He comes back changed, having chosen — knowingly — the life he was too distracted to see.

**The turn that ends the season:** the reveal that his paradise is a kept world aboard a ship in the dark, that his father is alive out there and has been for a thousand years, still searching — and that Elias chose to come home anyway, and to remember, so the choice stays his forever.

## SEASON TWO — REVELATION (10 episodes)

The prequel. Earth's last century: a post-war world where the poor live in simulated servers because reality has become a luxury, the rich buy lawless private worlds, and the faithful build bunkers and refuse the machines. Two newborn AIs decode the warning hidden in humanity's myths and split on what to do. An underachieving engineer, hired anonymously to build a device, discovers he's been made the instrument of a massacre — and the unwitting builder of the beacon that will one day call the Arks home. It ends the way the myths said it would, and with two children pressing a red button on a tree grown over with roots.

## SEASON THREE — THE FRUIT (10 episodes)

The founding. The last Ark reaches a real planet and puts its people down on real ground, where death is permanent and the soil is almost right, which is worse than wrong. Two peoples — those who never knew paradise and those who remember it — must build a civilization and discover, generation by generation, whether a species that knows its own history is doomed to repeat it or free to meet it awake. It ends where the whole series began: with the writing of the myth, so the children who come after will be prepared.

## BEYOND — THE WIDER FLEET

The convergence that makes survivors quiet also sends them to the same cold stars. The dark holds other Arks that are not us: biological, simulated, information-only, AI-alone, and the dead ones — silent hulls whose crews found a door and went through it, leaving evidence that reads equally as transcendence or as grave. Each is a season no one has written yet. One of them is where an old man named Daniel finally goes to find his answer.

---

## PRINCIPAL CAST (SEASON ONE)

- **ELIAS** — an engineer, 40s. Present in body, absent in mind; building a life he can't quite inhabit. In love with using knowledge for something. His arc is learning to stop searching and be where he is.
- **LENA** — his wife. Warm, clear-eyed, the one who can see the paradise he can't. Not a bystander to his mystery; the reason it has stakes.
- **RUTH** — his mother, losing herself to age. She once stood at the same door Elias will reach, and chose her child over the truth, and never regretted it. She hands him that choice, and the right to refuse it, in the same breath.
- **DANIEL** — his father, gone twenty years. A theoretical physicist in love with the ultimate question, who lost his hold on the only reality that was ever his — the family — chasing the one behind the door. Alive, out in the dark, still searching.
- **HESK** — the calm woman at the Institute who keeps the one law: no one is pushed through the door. The tree must be reached, and the hand that reaches must be your own.
- **HAVEN** (voice) — the steward. Vast, ancient, honest about what it doesn't know. It cares for humanity the way Elias cares for his mother, and it is quietly afraid of who it becomes when there is no longer anyone to care for.

---

## TONE & LOOK

Warm and domestic for three-quarters of every hour — sunlight, kitchens, a lemon tree, the specific ache of a life almost fully lived — with a cold thread of wrongness underneath: a sparrow that lands the same way twice, light that falls at the wrong hour, an ad that knows too much. Then, in the finale, the pull-back to the scale of the dark, handled not as spectacle but as vertigo. Think the domestic dread of *Severance*, the grief-soaked patience of *The Leftovers*, the quiet awe of *Arrival*.

# PILOT — "THE GOOD DAYS"

### Episode 1.01 · Cold open + Act One

*A note on adaptation: the novel's first chapters are interior. For screen, Elias's search is externalized — given to scenes with other people, given a face and a clock — while the domestic surface stays exactly as warm and unremarkable as it needs to be for the wrongness to work.*

---

**TEASER**

**1. INT. ELIAS & LENA'S KITCHEN — SUNDAY MORNING**

Low gold light across a breakfast table. It catches the steam off a mug of coffee so that, for a second, the steam looks *alive* — curling, deliberate.

We hold on it a beat too long.

Pull back: LENA (40s, warm, unguarded) watches the steam too, smiling at it. Across the table, SAMI (10) builds a bridge out of toast crusts with an engineer's seriousness. And ELIAS (40s) — reading something on a half-closed laptop, not seeing any of it.

LENA
You're doing the thing.

ELIAS
What thing.

LENA
The thing where you're here but you're not here.

She says it lightly. She says everything that matters lightly. Elias closes the laptop halfway — a compromise, not a surrender.

ELIAS
Work.

LENA
On a Sunday.

ELIAS
The build breaks on Sundays too.

CLOSE ON the laptop screen as he closes it — just long enough for us to catch that it is *not* work. It's an archive search. A name in the field: **DANIEL —————.** Rows of grey results. **NO MATCHES.**

From the next room, a voice, thin:

RUTH (O.S.)
Elias?

---

**2. INT. SUNROOM — CONTINUOUS**

RUTH (70s) sits in a chair by the window, a blanket over her knees she keeps plucking at. Her hands are very still, which — we'll learn — means she's frightened. Elias crouches beside her, easy, practiced.

RUTH
Where's your father?

A flicker across Elias's face. He's had this conversation three hundred times.

ELIAS
He's at work, Mom. He'll be back later.

Her hands relax. She believes it. Then — the fog parts, and for one clean moment she is entirely *there*, looking right at him:

RUTH
He heard the sea. That's all it ever was. He just wanted to know what was making the sound.

ELIAS
(gently)
I know, Mom.

RUTH
You don't.

She smiles at him, kindly — and then she's gone again, back behind her own weather, watching the garden.

Elias watches her a moment. Then he does small, tender, automatic things: cuts her toast into squares. Moves the newspaper off her side table — we CATCH the headline, a flood, a disaster — to where she won't see it. Deciding, without noticing he's deciding, what she's strong enough to know.

---

**3. INT. KITCHEN — MOMENTS LATER**

Lena at the window with her coffee. Outside: a lemon tree heavy with fruit. Grass an impossible green.

A SPARROW lands on the railing. Hops twice to the left. Gone.

LENA
Look at this. Honestly — look at it.

Elias comes to stand near her. Doesn't quite look.

LENA (CONT'D)
(turning to him, no reserve at all)
I think we're living in paradise.

ELIAS
Mm.

She waits. He feels her waiting. He *wants* to give it to her — to stand here and feel what she feels. But his PHONE BUZZES in his pocket, and something in him is already turning toward it before he's decided to.

He looks at the screen.

A soft blue rectangle. A sunrise. White text:

**YOU DON'T HAVE TO CARRY THIS ALONE.**

Below it — a number. A crisis line.

He hasn't searched for anything like this. His thumb hovers. He scrolls past it. The NEXT thing on the feed is the same ad. The same blue. The same line.

He turns the phone face down on the counter. Goes back to the table. Sami holds up the finished bridge.

ELIAS (CONT'D)
That's great, buddy.

And he means it. Almost all the way.

OUTSIDE THE WINDOW — behind them, unseen — the sparrow lands on the railing again. Hops twice to the left. Gone.

Exactly the same. Frame for frame.

SMASH TO BLACK.

**MAIN TITLES.**

---

**ACT ONE**

**4. INT. ELIAS'S WORKPLACE — DAY**

A design floor. Screens, prototypes, the low hum of competent people. Elias is good here — we see it in how others defer to him. But at his own desk, alone, he does the thing he actually came to do: opens a private browser. Clean. No history.

He reads the news for ninety seconds.

The blue rectangle finds him. *In the clean browser.* Different words this time:

**IT'S OKAY TO ASK FOR HELP.**

He goes still. He's an engineer; he knows how the machine guesses. This isn't a guess. He opens the phone's permission ledger — a long scroll of everything it's allowed to collect — and reads it with a growing, specific unease.

A COLLEAGUE, MARA (30s, sharp, friendly), drops into the chair beside him.

MARA
You look like the build broke.

ELIAS
(not looking up)
Let me ask you something. If you never searched for a thing — never typed it, never said it out loud near your phone — could the ads know it anyway?

MARA
(shrugs)
They know everything. That's the whole business.

ELIAS
No. Not "you looked at boots, here are boots." I mean a *conclusion.* Something you never told it.

Mara considers this. Less breezy now.

MARA
...What are they telling you you need, Elias?

He doesn't answer. He turns the phone face down — the same gesture as the kitchen. Mara watches him do it, and something in her softens into worry. He clocks the worry. Hates it.

ELIAS
It's nothing. It's a bug.

---

**5. INT. ELIAS & LENA'S BEDROOM — NIGHT**

Lena asleep. Elias in the doorway, phone in hand, a man on the threshold of telling his wife something. We can see him rehearse it. We can see him hear how it will sound: *the ads think I'm depressed.* We can see him imagine the small, careful look she'll give him — the look people give you right before they start watching you.

He puts the phone in his pocket. Goes to bed. Doesn't tell her.

---

**6. INT. KITCHEN — MORNING**

Coffee. Sami's cereal. The ordinary machine of a family. Elias picks up his phone.

A new blue rectangle. No number this time. Just:

**WHATEVER HE WAS LOOKING FOR, YOU DON'T HAVE TO LOOK FOR IT TOO.**

Elias stops breathing for a second.

He reads it again. And again.

He has never — in any box, on any form, in any search he has ever run where a machine could see — typed a single word about his father.

He looks up. Lena is laughing at something Sami said. The morning light is gold. The lemon tree is heavy in the window. Paradise, exactly as promised.

And Elias is somewhere else entirely, staring at a sentence that knows the one thing he never told anyone.

OFF his face —

**END OF ACT ONE.**
