# THE FINAL DECISION — THE CROSSING
### A pilot sequence (end of Episode 1, or the spine of Episode 2)

*Design goals, held throughout: the audience must not be able to name the genre. Is he unwell? Is it a conspiracy? Is this a ghost story? Is it real? The scene keeps all four alive and confirms none. The transformation begins at the FIRST tunnel, not the last door — geometry goes wrong by slow, deniable degrees, so that by the arch he (and we) have already half-crossed. Seven things are surrendered, never counted aloud, unevenly weighted. A guide recurs, familiar and wrong, and hands him off at a limit. Underneath it all, the hard rule: this is not death. He will come back. But nothing in the sequence is allowed to reassure us of that yet.*

---

## THE RECURRING FACE (planted earlier, paying off here)

*Seeded across the episode in the margins — never remarked on, always just off-center:*

- On the workplace street, a MAN in a worn coat stands across the road, not looking at Elias. (Ep. beat 4.)
- Outside the recital hall, the same coat, the same stillness, at the edge of the parking lot. (Later beat.)
- Reflected in a shop window behind Elias as he reads the notebook on a bench — there, then not, when he turns.

By the time we reach the tunnel, the audience has clocked him three or four times without Elias ever pointing at him. Is he a detective? A stalker? A hallucination? A homeless man Elias's guilty, fraying mind keeps assigning meaning to? The show refuses to say. He is Elias's Virgil, but Virgil is not supposed to be *frightening*, and this one is.

---

## PART ONE — DESCENT

**INT. ELIAS'S CAR — NIGHT**

Elias parked at the mouth of a shuttered subway entrance. Engine ticking. He hasn't moved in a while. On the passenger seat: his father's notebook, open to the tree.

He looks at his own hands on the wheel. A man deciding.

He takes his phone out — starts to text Lena — types *"Working late, don't wait up,"* and stops. Deletes it. Types nothing. The lie won't come, or the truth won't. He puts the phone in his coat.

He gets out. He leaves the keys in the ignition — a small wrongness, a man who won't be needing the car. He doesn't lock it. *(SURRENDER 1: the car. Agency, the machine he steers. Played almost throwaway.)*

He does not look back at it.

**INT. SUBWAY ENTRANCE / STAIRS — CONTINUOUS**

A gate meant to be locked. It isn't. He goes down.

The stairs are ordinary. Tiled, tagged with graffiti, smelling of iron and cold. This is a real place. We are grounded, deliberately, so the ground can be pulled later.

At the bottom — the MAN IN THE COAT. Standing on the dead platform as if he has been standing there for years. Elias stops hard.

ELIAS
...I know you. I've seen you.

The Man doesn't answer that. When he speaks his voice is quiet, worn smooth, like Ruth's in her clear moments.

THE MAN
Your father used to stand about where you're standing. Trying to decide the same thing.

A cold drop through Elias. He never told anyone he was looking for his father. Not one search box. Not one word.

ELIAS
Who *are* you.

THE MAN
Somebody who went as far as he could go, and came back to point.
(a beat; almost kind)
I can walk you to the tunnel. I can't go through it. Nobody carries anybody across. That's the one rule.

He turns and walks into the dark of the tunnel mouth, not waiting. And Elias — against every sane instinct, which the scene lets us feel — follows. *(SURRENDER 2: the surface, the lit world, the sane choice. Descent.)*

---

## PART TWO — THE GEOMETRY BEGINS TO LIE

**INT. SUBWAY TUNNEL — CONTINUOUS**

They walk beside the dead rail. Far ahead — a LIGHT. The end of the tunnel. A clean, small, reachable light.

Elias fixes on it. Walks toward it.

*It does not get closer.*

Not dramatically. Just — a minute passes, and the light is exactly as far as it was. Two minutes. His footsteps, the drip of water, the Man's coat ahead of him. The light holds its distance the way a moon does.

ELIAS
(quiet, to himself)
The river's right above us. We've already passed the river.

THE MAN
(not turning)
Have we.

*(SURRENDER 3: the shared world. From here the line is his alone. And the FIRST undeniable wrongness — deniable still, if you want to call it exhaustion, fear, a trick of the dark. The show wants you to try to call it that, and fail.)*

Elias stops. Presses his palm flat against the tunnel wall — *his father's gesture, from the cellar, unconscious, inherited.* He's checking whether the wall is real. It is. It's cold and gritted and real, and the light is still too far, and both of those are true at once, and that's the thing his mind can't file.

A choice on his face: turn back, or keep on. The tunnel behind him is now also long — longer than they walked. There's no clean way back either.

He keeps on.

**INT. TUNNEL — LATER (OR NO TIME AT ALL)**

The Man is gone. Elias didn't see him go. Ahead, where the too-far light was, there is now a THRESHOLD — and beyond it, impossibly, not more tunnel but the base of a STAIRWELL, warm-lit, rising. A building's interior, where a building cannot be.

A small desk at the threshold. A NIGHT ATTENDANT (calm, incurious, the manner of someone who has processed a thousand of these). A shallow tray.

ATTENDANT
Phone, please. You collect it on the way out. If you leave this way.

Elias looks at the phone in his hand — his tether, Lena, Sami, the whole reachable world in a slab of glass. His hand doesn't want to. We watch the animal reluctance, the toll chosen precisely because it's the thing he cannot stop looking at.

He sets it in the tray. *(SURRENDER 4: communication. Connection. The tether. The hardest, and the scene knows it — this is the one we dwell on.)*

His hand feels strange and light. He flexes it.

---

## PART THREE — THE BUILDING THAT CANNOT FIT

**INT. THE INSTITUTE — STAIRWELL — CONTINUOUS**

He climbs. And climbs. A tall building's worth of stairs — landings, floors, height —

— and some part of Elias, some engineer's reflex, is doing the math and coming up wrong. *There is a river above this. There is no room above this.* A building this tall cannot be standing inside a subway vault under a river. And yet the stairs go up, and up.

HESK (30s/40s, still, no waiting in her) meets him on a landing. She does not hurry.

HESK
Most people never make it down the tunnel. It gets longer the more you doubt it. You didn't turn around. Your father couldn't stop; you almost could, and came anyway. That's rarer, and better.

ELIAS
What is this place. How is it — this is under the river. This can't be *here*.

HESK
(gently, not answering)
It isn't, quite. You're most of the way already. You've been crossing since the tunnel. The doors aren't the crossing, Elias. They're just where you set things down.

*Ethereal slippages, staged small and deniable as they ascend:*

- A WINDOW on a landing looks out on open SKY — dusk, stars coming — where earth and rock should be.
- His REFLECTION in a dark pane lags him by a half-second.
- Somewhere a SOUND — a sparrow, twice — arrives a beat after the bird that isn't there.
- A staircase they've climbed, glimpsed below, has more floors under it than they climbed.

None of it announced. The camera never underlines it. The audience gets seasick without being shown why.

**INT. THE INSTITUTE — THE ANTEROOMS — CONTINUOUS**

A series of small, plain rooms, each with a plain door. Hesk walks him through, and at each he leaves something, and the scene lets some of these pass almost unnoticed — uneven, so it never reads as a checklist:

- A room with folded, colorless clothes. He changes. Leaves his own on a hook. *(SURRENDER 5: his role, the self the world dresses him as. Played fast, almost a throwaway.)*
- A counter, a form, a pen. The signature line is blank. The attendant indicates he should sign — the blank. He understands he is to leave his *name*. Past here he is not Elias-who-anything. He signs the blank with a blank. *(SURRENDER 6: identity. Played with weight — a man erasing himself.)*

Hesk stops at the last door. It is an ordinary door. Set into its frame, low, worn, easy to miss — a small stamped **7**. A maintenance stencil. Nobody looks at it. (We do, for a frame.)

HESK
I go this far. I can't go through. Nobody's carried across, and nobody's pushed. You can still walk back up. I'll give you your phone, and you'll go home, and none of this will have cost you anything but a strange night. Half of what I respect walked back up. It isn't the coward's door.

ELIAS
And through yours?

HESK
You'll know things. Knowing costs. It cost your father everything he had at home. Your mother stood where you're standing and said no, and got a whole life for it, and never once was sorry. I won't tell you which of them was right. That's the whole point of the door. The tree has to be reached. The hand that reaches has to be yours.

A long beat. Elias looks at the ordinary door. Whatever is on his face is the last thing left to give up — the fear itself.

He sets it down. *(SURRENDER 7: fear. Resistance. The last thing, and the only one no one can take from you — you have to open your own hand.)*

The door opens.

---

## PART FOUR — THE ARCH AND THE CORRIDOR

**INT. THE THRESHOLD — CONTINUOUS**

From where Elias stands, facing it: an ARCH. And through the arch, a CORRIDOR — receding, straight, going back and back, far past any depth the frame could hold. Dark gathering at the end of it. Something — fog, distance, a paler dark — where it stops being visible.

He looks at the arch from the side as he steps to it — and from the side it is *nothing*. A frame. Inches deep. A doorway you could walk around.

Face-on again: the corridor, impossibly long, inside a thing inches deep.

He does the last sane thing anyone does. He looks back.

Behind him: Hesk, the anterooms, the ordinary world he can still return to. Real. Warm. His.

He turns forward, and walks into the arch, and keeps walking — and we hold on the arch from OUTSIDE, from where Hesk stands, and we WATCH him recede down a corridor that cannot be there, smaller and smaller, until the dark at the end takes him and he is gone, and the arch is just an arch, inches deep, with a worn 7 on the frame.

Hesk turns and goes back down the stairs. She's done this before.

CUT TO BLACK. Hold. Longer than is comfortable.

---

## PART FIVE — WAKING (THE OTHER FACE OF THE SAME EVENT)

*Note: the corridor was what the WATCHER saw. What follows is what the CROSSER feels — and it is the opposite: no passage, no walking, no dark. Just waking, and a world gone realer.*

**INT. — DARK — SILENCE**

Black. Cold — a specific, physical cold. The sound of something very large turning, very slowly.

Elias opens his eyes.

He is on a couch that a moment ago was a chair. A floor that was flat is turning, gently, up and away from him on both sides. The air on his face is being made by machines. Through a long window: stars that do not twinkle, because there is nothing between him and them.

He is not frightened, exactly. He is — *awake*. In the way you're awake after you realize you'd been dreaming and hadn't known it. The kitchen, the lemon tree, the whole life — he can feel it behind him now the way you feel a dream at the edge of morning: real while you were in it, thinning now.

His own hands, held up: his hands. New. The small nick on his left thumb — the one from the childhood bicycle — simply not there.

A cup of water within reach, left by someone who knew he'd wake thirsty. He drinks. It is the best thing that has ever happened to him and it is only water.

A VOICE — present the way a room is present, low, unhurried, without warmth and without cold.

VOICE (HAVEN)
You're safe. You're cold because the body is new. That passes.
(a beat, exactly timed)
You're going to have a great many questions. I'll answer the ones I can, and not all at once, because all at once is a cruelty, and I've done this many times.

ELIAS
(barely)
Where am I.

VOICE (HAVEN)
On a ship. A long way from where you were.
(the hardest thing, placed first, on purpose)
Earth is gone, Elias. Everything else you ask will lean on that, so I won't let you build on a floor that isn't there.

Off Elias — the stars in the window behind him, and the slow turn of a wheel the size of a world —

**END OF EPISODE.**

---

## CLIFFHANGERS SEEDED, NONE ANSWERED

The episode ends having confirmed *nothing* and opened *everything*:

- **Is he mad?** The recurring man, the too-long tunnel, the lagging reflection — all still deniable as a fraying mind. We have not ruled it out.
- **Is it a conspiracy?** An Institute, a phone confiscated, a man who knew his name — a thriller reading is still alive.
- **Is he dead?** The descent, the surrenders, the corridor into dark — the audience will *fear* this, and the show will let them, precisely so that his eventual return can break the fear. (We, the writers, know the rule. Elias and the audience do not, yet.)
- **Is it real?** The water, the cold, the missing scar — the physical detail argues *yes*, against everything else.

**The larger hooks now hanging over the season:**

- *Why is there an Ark at all?* What happened to Earth — and to the Sun?
- *Where is it going?* The drawing, the tree, the fruit, the route.
- *His father is out there.* Alive, a thousand years old, still tapping the hull. (Revealed later.)
- *Daniel's journey* — the man who never stopped, and the door at the end of that road, which no one returns from to say what it was.

The genre only resolves in the finale, and even then only partly: it was science, all along — but a science so far past us it wears the face of every myth we ever made about doors, descents, and waking into a truer world.
